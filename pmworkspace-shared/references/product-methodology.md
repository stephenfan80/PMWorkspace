# Product Methodology

Use this as the general product-thinking foundation. It applies to internet products broadly: consumer, B2B, marketplace, content, internal tools, transactions, data products, and growth flows.

## Core Frame

Good prototype work starts from:

- **User problem:** the concrete pain, uncertainty, decision, task, or desire.
- **Current workaround:** what the user does today, even if manual, messy, or outside the product.
- **Product goal:** the behavior or outcome the team wants to improve.
- **Counter-metric:** the guardrail that prevents shallow metric wins.
- **Constraints:** business, legal, data, operations, design system, and implementation reality.
- **Value strategy:** what the user gets before and after any costly action.
- **Prototype promise:** what the screen must make clear enough to discuss, test, or hand off.

## Mature Product Questions

Ask selectively by scenario:

- Who is the specific user and what moment are they in?
- What job are they trying to finish?
- What would make them trust the next action?
- What do they already use instead?
- What is the smallest version that creates real value?
- What must the product not make worse?
- What information should be free because it is basic product value?
- What requires personalization, eligibility, freshness, saving, sharing, or human confirmation?
- What data is real today, and what is only a future capability?
- What should be previewed, deferred, or removed?

## Product Strategy Patterns

- **Preview before cost:** show useful value before asking for personal data, payment, account creation, install, or setup.
- **Result fulfills promise:** a result page must answer the reason the user acted.
- **Progressive precision:** move optional details after the first action when they improve accuracy but hurt momentum.
- **Decision-first dashboard:** start analytics pages from the decision, not the chart.
- **Task-first internal tools:** optimize repeated work, state visibility, and recovery over visual drama.
- **Service transparency:** show confirmed, pending, failed, and next-step states when fulfillment depends on people or partners.
- **No fake capability:** do not prototype unsupported automation, data, guarantees, or actions as if they exist.

## Scope Discipline

- Start with the smallest valuable prototype that can align the team.
- Offer bolder alternatives when they materially change user value, not when they only decorate the UI.
- Keep MVP and future expansion separate in the brief.
- If a feature is mostly a policy, data, or operations problem, make that visible instead of hiding it behind UI.

## Handoff Quality

A good prototype handoff includes:

- The user problem and scenario route.
- The target metric and counter-metric.
- The current baseline for existing features.
- The data and capability boundaries.
- The chosen concept direction and rejected directions.
- The screen list and per-screen job.
- The design system baseline.
- Open PM decisions.
